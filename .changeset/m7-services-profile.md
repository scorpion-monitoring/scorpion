M7 sprint 3: the Schema.org profile of a service, and the first **anonymous routes** of the product. A signed-in reader can fetch `GET /api/internal/services/{id}/schema-org` (a Schema.org `Service` as JSON-LD for any service; the provider is `provider`, the consortia are `brand`, the licence is the SPDX page; the stage and the contact point are never in it). A service becomes readable without signing in **only when a Reviewer or an administrator has made it public** (every service is still private until then): `GET /api/internal/public/services/{id or slug}` (an old slug works) and `/schema-org` answer with the public record (name, slug, logo, description, URL, `sameAs`, licence, stage, provider and consortia; no contact point, no member, no audit field), and `GET /api/internal/public/organisations/{id}` and `/schema-org` answer for an organisation that is the provider or a consortium of at least one public service (name, abbreviation, logo, description, website, ROR id, `sameAs` and its public services; no contact point and no member). For everything else, a private or unpublished service, an unknown id or slug, an organisation whose services are all private, and **every public address while the setting `service.allowPublic` is off**, the answer is the same 404 as for an id that does not exist, never a 403, so nobody can tell whether a private service exists. Switching `allowPublic` off is the operator's switch for all public addresses at once; it does not unpublish anything, and publishing is refused (409) while it is off. The public routes are rate limited like the rest of the API (the `default` group) and every successful answer says `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60`: an unpublish is seen at the next request by the server, and by a cache in front of it after at most a minute, so a reverse proxy or CDN that caches these routes adds at most that delay. When a Reviewer or an administrator makes a service public or private again, the managers of its provider and the person who created it get an inbox item and, unless they switched off the new notification category `registry-service`, a mail (English or German by their language) that names the service and the new state; the person who decided gets nothing. The JSON-LD block of the organisation page now comes from a shared component of the UI kit. Other modules can add Schema.org properties to a service profile through the registry `service.profileContributor` (an allow-list limits them), and `registry.organisations` gained the registry `org.publicReference` and the trusted read `listApprovedManagerIdsAsSystem`. No new setting, no migration.
